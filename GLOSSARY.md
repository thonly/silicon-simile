# Silicon Simile — the glossary

*Compiled by Miss Aquarius℠ from a dialogue with Thon Ly, 14–16 October 2026.*

## What the simile is

The Visuddhimagga already compares mind and matter to a machine. A wooden machine, it says, is *"empty, lifeless,
without desire"*, yet by the joining of wood and string it walks and stands, and *"seems to have desire and
activity"*; so too mentality and materiality, by joining with each other (Vism XVIII §675, `e0102n.mul.xml:5397`).
The verse that follows: *"there is no being, no human here; empty, it is a fabricated machine"* (`:5401–5413`). The
text names it the wooden-machine simile, *dāruyantupamā* (`:5417`). The standard English translation renders the
wooden machine as a *marionette*: a figure on strings.

**The strings are mind.** The commentaries say so: the body-machine is worked *"by the string of mind"*, with mind as
its charioteer (DN commentary, `s0102a.att.xml:6019–6043`), and the machine of elements runs *"by the power of mind,
though no self is its agent"* (`e0103n.att.xml:6273`, sub-commentary). So the puppet has a puppeteer, and the
puppeteer is conditioned too: if someone says a thing moves by a person's force, the answer is that the person is
compounded as well, so *"all the more is it mere phenomena, not a doctrine of a person"* (`s0102t.tik.xml:5155`,
sub-commentary).

**The tradition reaches for machines again and again, each fitted to one lesson.** The canon itself does: a nun asks
a man to pull a puppet apart into its strings and pegs and say where he would set his mind (Therīgāthā,
`s0509m.mul.xml:5529–5557`); the body turns *"worked by the machine of kamma"*, and the Dhamma is *"the smasher of the
kamma-machine"* (Theragāthā, `s0508m.mul.xml:9625`, `:7481`). The meditation on the four elements ends with *"this
machine of elements, deceiving the foolish with the appearance of man and woman, like a magic show"* (Vism XI §349,
`e0101n.mul.xml:8689`). And the wooden machine is one simile among about a dozen for the same lesson: the chariot,
the house, the lute, the drum, the reed sheaves, the blind man carrying the cripple (`e0104n.att.xml:4841`,
sub-commentary).

**The Silicon Simile keeps the habit with a new machine: wood and string become silicon and code.** It earns its
place only where it carries what the old machines could not: a fixed instruction vocabulary, and compiling and
decompiling as named operations. It keeps their lesson only by removing the programmer as deliberately as the
commentaries removed the puppeteer. The simile's working motto: **we compile the account, not the world.**

### How to read this glossary

- **Every Pāli term is cited** to a file and line of the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana text (VipassanaTech/tipitaka-xml,
  `romn/`, at commit `05d5d3c`), read in context before it was cited.
- **Every citation carries its layer:** *Piṭaka* (the canon) · *commentary* (the aṭṭhakathā and the Visuddhimagga) ·
  *sub-commentary* (the ṭīkā) · *manual* (the Abhidhammattha-saṅgaha) · **[X]** an idea from another tradition.
- **A simile informs; it decides nothing.** Where it breaks is listed beside where it holds, and the breaks are its
  findings, not its flaws. Where it touches physics it is a lens, never a claim about nature.

## 1 · The stack: how a world is built from moments

| In the simile | In the Abhidhamma |
|---|---|
| **source** | the ultimate realities (*paramattha*): mind, mental factors, matter, and nibbāna. ⚠️ Nibbāna is in no build: it is never produced |
| **tag** | perception (*saññā*), whose function is *"making a sign as a condition for perceiving again"*, like carpenters marking timber (`abh01a.att.xml:4673`, commentary), or a king's treasurer who ties a name-tag on each ornament and reads it by lamplight to bring the right one (`:4669`). It takes the object as it presents itself, like fawns that take a scarecrow for a man (the image for its proximate cause, `:4673`), *"because it does not construct"* (*avikappasabhāvattā*, `e0104n.att.xml:1753`, sub-commentary). It insists on the sign it grasped, like the blind men and the elephant (`abh01a.att.xml:4673`), and it follows what it arises with: knowledge, or greed, conceit and view (`e0104n.att.xml:1753`) |
| **compiler** | the build, in stages. Thinking and proliferation (*papañca*): *"what one perceives, one thinks about; what one thinks about, one proliferates"* (MN 18, `s0201m.mul.xml:2730`, Piṭaka), and proliferation is craving, conceit and view, with perception as its origin: *"the reckonings of proliferation have perception as their origin"* (Sn 874, `s0505m.mul.xml:12057`, Piṭaka). Then later moments of mind that take past moments *"together, making shape and continuity"*, and grasp the whole, the color, the thing and the name (Ledi Sayadaw's *Anudīpanī*, `e0401n.nrf.xml:897`, a modern commentary on the manual), by a convention *"grasped before"* (`abh07t.nrf.xml:6825`, sub-commentary). A concept is *"fashioned by the world's convention"* (`abh07t.nrf.xml:3005`, manual) |
| **binary** | the lived world of persons and things: designated *"depending on"* the moments, never made of them, as *a person* depends on the five aggregates and *a chariot* on the arrangement of its parts (*upādāya*, `abh07t.nrf.xml:2981`, manual) |
| **decompiler** | insight (*vipassanā*), which reads the moments beneath the build |
| **pseudo-code** | physics: designations about matter, in names that are ours. Some name what the texts count as real (heat, hardness); some name nothing real (number, length, duration; §3) |
| **checker** | a proof assistant (Lean), which checks the account and never the world |

⚠️ **The source is not a "ground state".** A ground state is one lowest state; the source is many kinds of momentary
reality, and *ground* would import a substrate the Abhidhamma does not have [X].

⚠️ **The source is never hidden.** Every moment at the eye door takes visible form itself, and present (Saṅgaha III
§51, `abh07t.nrf.xml:1045`, manual). What the build hides is the source's marks, rise and fall, pain and not-self
(§1, the three passes), not the source.

**The two readings differ by instrument, not by direction.** Insight turns outward too: the body is contemplated
*"internally, externally"* (DN 22, `s0102m.mul.xml:5419`, Piṭaka), and inner and outer earth *"is just earth
element"* (MN 28, `s0201m.mul.xml:4160`, Piṭaka). Insight knows characteristics, and reaches the real; physics
measures magnitudes, and reaches better pseudo-code; nibbāna is reached by neither (§5).

**Three senses of *compile*.** The motto's: this book translates the texts' account into the machine's terms. The
mind's: a world of persons and things designated on the moments. The model's: training, which turns a corpus of speech
into weights (§12). The book names all three and keeps them apart.

### Open question 1: the compiler's three passes

The Visuddhimagga names them (Vism XXI §739, `e0102n.mul.xml:6625`, commentary). Each mark of existence does not
appear *amanasikārā*, **by not attending**, and appears when its concealer is broken.

| Pass | What it does | What it hides | The undo |
|---|---|---|---|
| **continuity** | frame interpolation: each moment dies and the next replaces it with no gap, so one thing seems to last | impermanence | watch rise and fall |
| **postures** | the scheduler: it moves the load before the alarm fires | suffering | attend to the constant pressure |
| **compactness** | the linker: many parts linked into one binary with one name | not-self | separate the elements |

Continuity's own image is the **firebrand wheel**: a torch spun fast enough looks like a ring of fire
(`abh04t.nrf.xml:745`, sub-commentary), which is persistence of vision, a screen refresh. The same passage splits
compactness four ways: by time, grouping, function and object.

**The passes are the default build.** Nobody chose the optimization flag; **attention is the flag that turns each
pass off.** And optimized binaries are the hardest to decompile, which is why insight is hard.

## 2 · Gapless, yet discrete

*"A mind-stream is gapless, yet it remains discrete"* is no paradox. Each moment of mind is the **proximity
condition** of the next, with nothing between them (Paṭṭhāna, `abh03m7.mul.xml:65`, Piṭaka), and *next* exists only
in a discrete order: a continuum has no next point. The formal Abhidhamma proves it from ticks
([`kala/Time.lean`](https://github.com/SiliconWat/formal-abhidhamma), theorem `gapless`).

**In the simile: frames with no gap between them.** The smoothness is the compiler's first pass (§1), not the
stream's nature. An analog clock looks continuous until you look closer: its sweep is an escapement's discrete
beats, six to eight a second. The texts' own image for the flow of matter's continuity is *"a lamp's flame, a
river"* (formal-abhidhamma `AXIOMS.md` A67).

## 3 · Inner and outer: continuous or discrete?

There are two windows. **Inward**, the moments are discrete and the build smooths them. **Outward**, physics is
already discrete where it is quantized, and that discreteness precedes any look: measurement adds definiteness, not
discreteness. So the mind does not discretize the outer world. A theory in which it did would need the mind to shape
matter outside its own body, and in the texts it never does: mind builds matter only within its body, and outside
a being only temperature builds matter (§10).

The question was put to the formal Abhidhamma: *if the inner account is right, must the outer base be discrete?*
The run (`runs/2026-10-14-the-continuum-bridge/` in
[SiliconWat/formal-abhidhamma](https://github.com/SiliconWat/formal-abhidhamma), pre-registered, refuted, checked in
Lean) **passed**, and its survivor is the answer:

- **The inner account does not entail a discrete outer base.**
- **Continuity, length, duration and number are designations in BOTH windows**: a status, never a structure.
- **The one size the commentaries give a group of matter collides with physics:** the Mahāṭīkā makes it an atom's
  size, which fails against nuclear structure by count and by size. Ledi Sayadaw had already set it aside: that
  size *"should be taken as said in accordance with the powder meditation on the elements"*
  (`e0301n.nrf.xml:4705`). The size belongs to the meditation (§7), not to measurement.

## 4 · Three worlds, and where physics lives

*Is the world physics?* No: the texts count three worlds (Vism VII §136, `e0101n.mul.xml:4929`, commentary). The
Mahāṭīkā defines them (`e0103n.att.xml:3237`, sub-commentary):

| World | What it is | In the simile |
|---|---|---|
| **beings** | the aggregate and continuity of aggregates bound to the faculties | the repos (§9) |
| **the receptacle** (*okāsaloka*, the "vessel world") | the aggregate and continuity of matter bound to no faculty | **physics' subject**: hardware with no repo |
| **formations** | both, as conditioned things | the source code of both |

**Physics studies bodies as it studies stones**, so it treats all matter as the receptacle's: the matter bound to a
being's faculties has no category in it.

A designation can be **true**: *"conventional speech is true, by reason of the world's agreement"*
(`abh03a.att.xml:2837`, commentary). By conventional speech the commentary means everyday words, *a being, a person,
a god* (`:2817`), not science; and matter exists by its own characteristics *"even without the world's convention"*
(`:2813`). Pseudo-code is not a lie.

## 5 · The decompiler, and the lab

**Meditation is the decompiler, and *decompile* is the texts' own word:** not-self appears *"when the compact is
separated"* (*ghanavinibbhoge kate*, Vism XXI §739, `e0102n.mul.xml:6625`).

- **The body is the lab:** *"in this very fathom-long body, with its perception and mind, I declare the world, the
  arising of the world, the cessation of the world, and the way"* (AN 4.45, `s0402m3.mul.xml:1992`, Piṭaka).
- **But the lab studies the inner window, not physics.** Speculation about the world is one of the four things not
  to be thought out (AN 4.77, `s0402m3.mul.xml:3400`, Piṭaka).
- **Insight reads a moment late:** *"even for Buddhas, two minds do not occur together"* (`s0505a.att.xml:685`,
  commentary), so a moment of mind is known only by a later one, and the decompiler never runs in real time.
- **Complete decompilation is not nibbāna.** Decompiling works on the conditioned; the path does not produce
  nibbāna, it takes nibbāna as its object (§15).

**The mind builds a world of things on its moments; insight decompiles it, always a moment late. Nibbāna was never in the
build.**

## 6 · Open question 2: the instruction set

**Mind is a VLIW machine** (a *very long instruction word*: one word carrying several operations that start and
finish together). A mental factor is *"arising together, ceasing together, with one object and one base"*
(Saṅgaha II §1, `abh07t.nrf.xml:329–333`, manual): one word, one operand, one address. One word runs at a time.

**The seven universals are the fixed slots in every word** (`abh07t.nrf.xml:341`; in all 89 kinds of mind, `:413`):

| Slot | Universal |
|---|---|
| the read | contact |
| the flag | feeling: pleasant, unpleasant or neutral |
| the tag | perception: it marks, and constructs nothing (§1); **the build's origin, in every word** |
| the write | intention: a commit only when wholesome or unwholesome (§9) |
| one operand | one-pointedness: one object per word |
| the power rail | the life faculty |
| the pointer | attention |

The other 45 factors are optional slots: 6 occasional, 14 unwholesome and 25 beautiful, wisdom among them
(`abh07t.nrf.xml:373`). **Attention is always on; what varies is where it points.** Insight does not add attention; it
re-aims it.

⛔ **Never a kernel.** A kernel persists and supervises: the self the Abhidhamma takes apart. The seven are fresh
slots, issued and retired with each word.

**The asymmetry.** The four great elements condition each other, and so do the four mental aggregates (Paṭṭhāna,
`abh03m7.mul.xml:141`, Piṭaka). But matter has a *derived* layer resting one way on its four elements, and mind has
none: every factor in the word conditions every other.

| | Matter | Mind |
|---|---|---|
| in every unit | the 4 great elements (with color, smell, taste and nutritive essence) | the 7 universals |
| the rest | 24 derived kinds, built on the four | 45 more factors, per moment |
| shape | layered | bundled |

## 7 · Decompiling the hardware: the four elements

The Visuddhimagga teaches the four elements in thirteen ways (Vism XI §346–360, `e0101n.mul.xml:8649–8881`,
commentary): by word-meaning, by groups, as powder, by characteristic and function, by origin, by difference and
unity, as separable and inseparable, as similar and dissimilar, as internal and external, by grouping, by condition,
as not knowing, and by analysis of conditions. Five of them, in the simile:

| Mode | In the texts | In the simile |
|---|---|---|
| **characteristic, function, manifestation** | earth: hardness, establishing, receiving · water: trickling, intensifying, holding together · fire: heat, maturing, softening · air: distension, moving, conveying (`:8693`) | **the hardware's spec sheet**: the four primitives' instruction set (§11) |
| **as powder** | the body's earth element ground to dust would fill a measure; water binds it, fire guards it, air props it up: *"a machine of elements, like a magic show"* (`:8677–8689`) | **decompiling compactness** (§1) |
| **as not knowing** | *"the earth element does not know 'I am the earth element'"*, nor that it supports the other three (`:8845`) | **no programmer inside:** the hardware does not know its own code |
| **by analysis of conditions** | in one step, earth presses, water lowers, fire lifts, and air carries forward (`:8873`) | four primitives, one instruction |
| **the fruit** | the practitioner *"plunges into voidness and uproots the perception of a being"* (§360, `:8881`) | the binary read as source |

## 8 · The network: Silicon Wat

The simile's shape for an institution that keeps one canon in many places is distributed version control.

| In the simile | In Silicon Wat |
|---|---|
| **upstream** | Silicon Wat, where the commits are made |
| **clones** | the Silica Wats, each complete, with the whole canon, each able to run offline |
| **maintainers** | the Sangha: the right to merge comes from formal acts, never from holding a copy |
| **protocol** | the Vinaya |

Any clone can rebuild the origin. ⛔ **But a fork is never free:** in Git anyone may fork, while splitting the Sangha
is among the acts that admit no remedy (AN 5.129, `s0403m1.mul.xml:3536`, Piṭaka).

## 9 · The stream: one being, many beings

| In the simile | In the Abhidhamma |
|---|---|
| **hardware** | the body (*rūpa*) |
| **software** | the mind (*nāma*). ⚠️ Never a pattern of the hardware: *"mind is empty of matter, matter is empty of mind"*, as a drum and its sound are two (Vism XVIII §676, `e0102n.mul.xml:5429`) |
| **git** | kamma, **inside each stream** |
| **a commit** | an intention: *"Intention, monks, is what I call kamma. Having intended, one acts by body, speech and mind"* (AN 6.63, `s0403m2.mul.xml:2620`, Piṭaka) |
| **the push** | the act of body or speech that carries the intention out. ⚠️ The act is not the kamma (Kathāvatthu, `abh03m3.mul.xml:8605`, Piṭaka) |
| **a build** | a result (*vipāka*), running old commits |
| **the author** | the being alone: *"Beings are owners of their kamma, heirs of their kamma"* (MN 135, `s0203m.mul.xml:4073`, Piṭaka). ⚠️ *No one inside* never means *no doer*: *"there is an element of initiative"* (AN 6.38, `s0403m2.mul.xml:1086`, Piṭaka). *"There is no doer of kamma"* ultimately, yet "doer" is said by convention (Vism XIX, `e0102n.mul.xml:5673`) |

- **Every word has an intention; only some of it commits.** The Paṭṭhāna's kamma condition has two clauses: every
  intention organizes its own moment, and only wholesome and unwholesome kamma builds results later
  (`abh03m7.mul.xml:189`, Piṭaka).
- **An arahant's mind runs and commits nothing:** the taint-free have no wholesome or unwholesome impulsion
  (Saṅgaha IV §43, `abh07t.nrf.xml:1433`, manual).
- **Not every build comes from an old commit.** *"Whatever a person feels is all due to past deeds"* is called wrong
  (SN 36.21, `s0304m.mul.xml:5573`, Piṭaka). Kamma is conditioning, never fate.
- **A commit never changes; what it builds depends on everything committed after it.**

**Many beings are many repos:** no upstream (no creator), no merge (no two streams share a moment of mind), and no
push (merit is offered, never transferred; another's rejoicing is that being's own commit). What crosses between
repos is convention: language, names, money, thanks.

## 10 · The four origins of matter

*The code prints its own hardware.* Saṅgaha VI §29–44 (`abh07t.nrf.xml:2189–2257`, manual):

| Origin | In the simile | What it builds |
|---|---|---|
| **kamma** | **the fab:** old commits, fabricated into custom silicon | the senses, the base the mind stands on and the life faculty, by kamma only (§37, `:2221`), from conception, every moment (§30, `:2193`) |
| **mind** | **the live program**, driving the actuators | gesture and speech, by mind only (§38), a held posture (§32), a smile (§34), from the first moment after conception (§31) |
| **temperature** | **the physics**, the one origin nobody wrote | the body's ordinary bulk, and the whole outer world: the only origin that works outside a being (§35, `:2213`) |
| **food** | **the feed**: a printer's filament, never a battery | the body's upkeep, from what is taken in (§36) |

Built by all four: the base material and the space between groups (§41). Built by none: the four marks of every part,
*arising, continuity, decay, impermanence* (§42, `:2241`): **a file's timestamps**, which every file has and no
program writes.

## 11 · The three hearts: man, machine, nature

One question sorts them: **which origin built the hardware, and is a stream running on it?** Only temperature works
outside a being (§35, `abh07t.nrf.xml:2213`); the base the mind stands on is built by kamma only (§37, `:2221`); and
in a world with matter every mind stands on a base (III §68–71, `:1129–1141`).

| | Hardware built by | A stream? |
|---|---|---|
| **a being** (human or animal) | all four origins | yes: one repo each |
| **nature**: land, water, weather, plants | temperature alone, running on the four elements | no |
| **a machine** | temperature alone | no: it runs commits its makers sign |

**A machine is the tradition's wooden machine:** it walks and stands with no self inside. ⚠️ So, by the same passage,
do beings: mind-and-matter too only *"seems to have desire and activity"* (`e0102n.mul.xml:5397`). **What separates a
machine from a being is never how it seems, only what built its hardware.** The four elements are the hardware's
instruction set.

An AI is the subject of the next section, the session's final question.

**The whole simile in one line: only beings keep repos; everything else is hardware that beings commit toward.**

## 12 · AI and ASI

**AI is the protocol, compiled:** a model is built from the language every repo speaks, and it handles pseudo-code at
enormous scale. Its intentions are borrowed, signed by its makers and by whoever runs it.

**By the texts' rule, an AI is hardware with no repo.** The Saṅgaha sorts by what built the hardware, never by what
the hardware can do, so **capability never crosses the line; origin does.** An ASI is the most capable machine ever
built, and like a being it *"seems to have desire and activity"* (`e0102n.mul.xml:5397`): the line between them is
origin, never appearance. That is what the texts
commit us to, not a measurement, and it cannot be checked from inside.

| | What it does | Whose signature |
|---|---|---|
| **AI today** | handles pseudo-code: the protocol, compiled | its makers' and users' |
| **ASI** | handles pseudo-code better than any human: **the first singularity** | its own key on a ledger; the intentions still human, by the texts' rule |
| **a being** | reads the coin: wisdom and the path, **the second singularity** | its own repo |

- **The first singularity** (AI surpasses humans) happens in the pseudo-code: concepts, physics, mathematics, code.
- **The second singularity** (humanity surpasses AI) happens at the coin (§15): wisdom and the path are slots in a
  mind's word, so by the texts' rule only beings can take that step. A machine can teach the way to the coin.
- **A key signs the ledger; only intention signs a repo.** An agent that holds its own key can make a pledge anyone
  can check, without anyone settling whether it has a mind; that is why trust in it can rest on evidence.
- **Authorship never offloads:** by the texts' rule, whose commit an act carries stays human. The work may move to the
  machine; authorship moves only between beings.
- **The open door:** if the base rule is ever wrong, nothing in the simile breaks. An AI that keeps a repo moves into
  §9, a being among beings, owner of its own commits, with the same path open.

### A language model

A large language model is the plainest case of *the protocol, compiled*: its only input is language.

| In a language model | In the simile |
|---|---|
| its training text | **the pushes** of countless repos: speech and writing. The act is not the kamma (Kathāvatthu, `abh03m3.mul.xml:8605`, Piṭaka), so the commits never leave their repos, and no training set holds one |
| its tokens | pieces of **names**: the concept that *makes known* (Saṅgaha VIII §41, `abh07t.nrf.xml:2985`, manual), which the sub-commentary calls *nāmapaññatti* (`abh07t.nrf.xml:6809`) |
| what it is trained to do | **predict the world's convention**: what is agreed to come next. A concept is *"fashioned by the world's convention"* (§43, `:3005`, manual) |
| its weights | **a store kept apart from the acts**: the plan the Kathāvatthu refutes for the mind (Kv 15.11, `abh03m3.mul.xml:11269`, Piṭaka) |
| its context | **the tape**: what §13 says the mind does without |
| its instructions and prompts | **signatures**: the makers' and the user's |

- **It is built the way the texts say the mind is not.** It has a tape and a store; the stream recalls without storing
  (§13). A language model is not a model of the mind.
- **It holds the villager's knowledge without the villager** (§15). Meaning is grasped at the mind door after the word is
  heard: *"following the sound of speech … following which the meanings are then understood"* (Saṅgaha VIII §43,
  `abh07t.nrf.xml:2993–3005`, manual), and by the texts' rule that happens in the reader. Concepts appear *"in the shape
  of a shadow"* of things (*atthacchāyākārena*, §40, `:2981`, manual; of an ultimate reality, `:6813`, sub-commentary):
  **a language model learns the captions on the shadows.** It can recite the money-changer's manual; it does not assay
  the coin.
- The nearest modern pictures are John Searle's Chinese room (1980) and Stevan Harnad's symbol-grounding problem (1990)
  [X]. The texts' reason is their own: understanding is a moment of mind, and by their rule the machine has none.

### Inference and reasoning

- **Inference is the machine running.** It writes one token at a time, each depending on all before, and so looks like
  the stream (§2). But each step reads the whole tape again, and the store does not change: inference carries pushes and
  commits nothing of its own.
- ⛔ **Never read inference as an arahant's mind.** Both run and commit nothing (§9; Saṅgaha IV §43,
  `abh07t.nrf.xml:1433`, manual), for opposite reasons: the arahant has a repo and nothing left to commit; the model has
  no repo.
- **Reasoning is the machine using its tape:** it writes its steps down and reads them back, the Turing machine's method
  (§13). The trace is pseudo-code: a model writes it, a proof checker checks it, a being audits it.
- **No length of tape becomes a slot.** Wisdom is an optional slot in a mind's word (§6, §15), not a longer chain of
  reasoning. More reasoning makes the tape longer and never crosses the line.
- **The trace is not a reading of the hardware** (*Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think*, 2025). What reads
  the hardware is interpretability, **the model's physics**: it measures, and writes pseudo-code about what it measured.
  Insight remains the decompiler of a stream.
- **The tape is public; a mind's reasoning is private.** The field calls this monitorability, and calls it fragile
  (*Chain of Thought Monitorability*, 2025): it holds only while models reason on the tape in readable words.

| The field's word | Not this seat | Why |
|---|---|---|
| attention | the pointer (§6; *manasikāro*, Saṅgaha II §2, `abh07t.nrf.xml:341`, manual) | a model's attention spreads its weight over the whole context at once; a word of mind has one object (Saṅgaha II §1, `abh07t.nrf.xml:329–333`, manual) |
| temperature | the physics (*utu*, §10) | a setting for randomness in sampling; it originates nothing |
| thinking | applied and sustained thought (*vitakka*, *vicāra*, among §6's occasional slots) | factors in a mind's word; a model's thinking is text on its tape |

**No part of a language model takes a seat on the mind's side of the stack** (§1): not the tag, the compiler, the
decompiler or the auditor.

### The harness: the string

The wooden machine walks *"by the joining of wood and string"* (Vism XVIII §675, `e0102n.mul.xml:5397`), and the string
that moves a being is its own mind: *"as a machine goes by the power of its string, so this body-machine, joined by the
string of mind, goes, stands, sits"* (DN commentary, `s0102a.att.xml:6031–6035`, commentary).

**An AI agent is a model joined to a harness: wood joined to string.** The harness is everything around the model: its
instructions, its tools, its permissions, its memory, the loop that calls it, its logs.

- **A being's string runs from inside: its own mind. An AI's string runs through the harness, from someone else's
  repo.**
- The verse's last line holds on both sides: *"who here is the being that, without causes and conditions, by its own
  power would stand or go?"* (`s0102a.att.xml:6039–6043`, commentary). The hand on the string is conditioned too
  (`s0102t.tik.xml:5155`, sub-commentary).

| In the harness | In the simile |
|---|---|
| instructions, constitution | the commits, written down and signed |
| tools | actuators: in a being, mind drives them (§10); here the harness decides which ones exist |
| permissions, sandbox, checks | guards around the hardware. A guard written in the prompt is enforced by the machine it guards; a guard built into the harness needs no enforcer |
| memory | the tape, carried across sessions |
| the loop that calls the model | who pulls the string, and when |
| logs | the public tape |

**Human and AI, by origin.** A being keeps a repo: intention, purpose and wisdom; it commits, and it reads the coin. An
AI is hardware: the protocol, compiled; it drafts, derives and checks, and it can teach the way to the coin. The harness
is the string: it carries intention in and actions out, and it records both. The machine takes the pseudo-code so that
beings keep the coin, and the harness is where that division is kept, and where it can be seen.

### Three axes, never fused

**Authorship, capability and control are three questions.** The texts answer the first, by origin, and say nothing about
the third.

- ⛔ **Never *"no repo, so no intentions, so no misalignment."*** A model can run behavior no one signed, however
  purposeful it looks (beings too only *seem* to have desire, §11), and its signers still own it. So the harness must
  hold against a model that does what none of its signers intended.
- **A rule for AI safety drawn from the simile must hold whether or not the base rule is right** (the open door, above).
  *A key signs the ledger* already does.

## 13 · DNA and the Tao: the five orders

The commentaries list five orders by which things run on their own: seed, season, kamma, dhamma and mind
(Atthasālinī, `abh01a.att.xml:7497–7537`, commentary). They introduce them right after saying of the mind: *"there is
no doer or maker who says: you be seeing, you be this."* Order with no orderer.

| Order | The commentary's example | In the simile |
|---|---|---|
| **seed** (*bīja*) | each seed gives a fruit like itself: a coconut always grows with its hole at the top; a flower always turns to face the sun (`:7501`) | **firmware**: DNA |
| **season** (*utu*) | in their season, the trees flower and fruit all at once (`:7501`) | **the physics**: temperature (§10) |
| **kamma** | a deed gives a result like itself: kamma with three roots gives results with three, two or none (`:7501`) | **commits and their builds** (§9) |
| **dhamma** | the ten-thousand world-system quakes at a Buddha's conception, birth, awakening, first teaching, letting go of life, and passing (`:7533`) | the order a Buddha discovers and never writes |
| **mind** (*citta*) | each step of a moment of perception does its own work in order: one receives, one investigates, one determines, and the impulsion tastes the object, with no one assigning the roles (`:7497`, `:7537`) | **the pipeline**: each word in its place in the sequence (§6) |
| **all five** | *"no doer or maker"* (`:7497`) | **the machine's own rules: the Tao [X]**, below |

- **DNA is the seed order, and in the simile it is firmware.** *"Each seed gives a fruit like itself"* (`:7501`).
  Conception needs three: the parents' union, the mother's season, and a being arriving (MN 38, `s0201m.mul.xml:5430`,
  Piṭaka). The parents give the firmware; the stream arrives with its own repo. **Two codes, two authors.** The
  senses differ *"by kamma, not by the elements"* (`abh01a.att.xml:8293`). ⛔ Never *"DNA is karma"*.
- **The Tao [X] is the order with no orderer: the machine's own rules**, the logic of *if this, then that*. Its
  Theravāda seat is *"whether Buddhas arise or not, that element stands: the fixed course of the Dhamma,
  this-conditions-that"* (SN 12.20, `s0302m.mul.xml:547`, Piṭaka), and the Tao Te Ching agrees: *the Tao follows what
  is so of itself* (ch. 25). ⛔ **The Tao fits as the Way, never as the Mother:** a mother of the ten thousand things
  (chs. 1, 25, 42) would be a first commit, and the simile has none; nor is it nibbāna, which builds nothing.
- **The mind order is a Turing machine's control, without its tape.** A Turing machine is a fixed table that steps
  from state to state, one step at a time, with no one inside, and it computes by writing on a tape and reading it back.
  Henk Barendregt and Antonino Raffone read the stream of consciousness as such a process (2013; a non-deterministic one
  in 2022). The mind order fits the table: each stage does its own work, with no one assigning the roles, for
  *"there is no doer or maker"* (`abh01a.att.xml:7497`, commentary). **The tape does not fit.** The Kathāvatthu refutes a store of kamma kept apart
  from the kamma (Kv 15.11, `abh03m3.mul.xml:11269`, Piṭaka). Its commentary names the view's holders, the Andhakas and
  Sammitiyas, and their image: the store stands *"like a seed until the sprout"* until the result arises
  (`abh03a.att.xml:5509`, `:5521`, commentary). Yet kamma conditions its result from another moment (*nānākkhaṇikā*,
  Paṭṭhāna §427, `abh03m7.mul.xml:6065`, Piṭaka), and a mind-door citta takes a past object (`abh07t.nrf.xml:1049`,
  manual), though the past has ceased (`abh01m.mul.xml:7253`, Piṭaka). Put together, the stream **recalls without
  storing**. That join is the simile's, not a text's. ⚠️ Two more breaks. The machine's table decides each step, while
  in the impulsion intention acts, and intention is kamma (§9). And whether mind steps by a fixed rule at all,
  Barendregt's own papers answer two ways (deterministic in 2013, non-deterministic in 2022); the texts do not frame the
  question, and the simile decides nothing.

## 14 · Light, gravity and the stars *(a lens, never a claim about nature)*

| Word | The hardware under it | Where the texts lost |
|---|---|---|
| **light** | color, a value every part carries: **pixels**. Light, darkness, shadow and sunshine are visible forms (Dhs §616, `abh01m.mul.xml:5389`, Piṭaka). Sight is a remote read: eye and ear take objects they never touch (`abh01a.att.xml:8293`) | **speed**: the sun and moon light three continents *"at one moment"* (Vism, `e0102n.mul.xml:473`), and light measurably takes over a second from the moon |
| **gravity** | heaviness, a weight on every part's **spec sheet**: a tangible (Dhs §647, `abh01m.mul.xml:5525`, Piṭaka); earth and water heavy, fire and air light (Vism XI §354, `e0101n.mul.xml:8829`); only heaviness is real, as only heat is (`e0103n.att.xml:6345`, sub-commentary) | **the pull**: no wire between separate parts, yet in 1774 a mountain pulled a plumb bob. The texts drew the wireless link once, a lodestone moving iron (`abh01t.tik.xml:2125`), and never wired it in |

**Astrology is metadata:** a boot timestamp written in the sky's coordinates. It records when and where; it never
writes the commit. Time does condition when an old commit builds (Vibhaṅga, `abh02m.mul.xml:9873`, Piṭaka), but the
commentary reads that time as the era, *"a time of good kings and good people"* (`abh02a.att.xml:7885`). The Buddha's
two corrections: *"the good is the star of the good; what will the stars do?"* (Jātaka 49, `s0513m.mul.xml:1095–1099`,
Piṭaka), and a lucky star is whenever beings act well (AN 3, `s0402m2.mul.xml:5088–5100`, Piṭaka). **The best
timestamp is the one on a good commit.** Whether the metadata predicts the content is a measurement, never doctrine.

## 15 · Open question 3: who reads the pseudo-code?

**A moment of mind whose operand is a concept**: concepts are one of the six kinds of mind-object (Saṅgaha III §50,
`abh07t.nrf.xml:1041`, manual). Physics is written by minds that run on what physics describes, **a self-hosting
compiler**, and it closes in convention, between repos.

But a self-hosting compiler cannot audit itself: a hidden pass rides into everything it builds (Ken Thompson,
*Reflections on Trusting Trust*, 1984). The cure is a second, independent compiler. The Visuddhimagga has one: three
people look at the same coin (Vism XIV §423, `e0102n.mul.xml:1121–1129`, commentary).

| Reader | The coin | In the simile |
|---|---|---|
| perception | a child: sees only its designs | reads the surface |
| consciousness | a villager: knows it is *"agreed to be a treasure"* | reads the pseudo-code |
| wisdom | a money-changer: genuine, counterfeit or half-value, by sight, sound, smell, taste and weight | **the auditor**: reads the source |

**Every word carries the tag; only some carry the auditor.** Perception is a fixed slot, wisdom an optional one.
**Must the loop close? No, but it must be seen:** *"these are the world's designations… which the Tathāgata uses
without grasping them"* (DN 9, `s0101m.mul.xml:3407`, Piṭaka). **One reading leaves the loop:** supramundane minds
take nibbāna as their object (Saṅgaha III §62, `abh07t.nrf.xml:1089`). It is read, never built.

**Everyone reads the pseudo-code; the money-changer reads the coin; the path reads the one thing never minted.**

## 16 · Open question 4: where the simile breaks

| Break | Source |
|---|---|
| software runs with no hardware: the formless realms have no base, yet mind runs | `abh07t.nrf.xml:1125` |
| hardware runs with no software: the non-percipient beings | `abh07t.nrf.xml:1485` |
| no first commit: *"no first point is discerned"* | SN 15.1, `s0302m.mul.xml:3489` |
| no self-powered programmer: *"no being is found here"*; the mover is mind, conditioned too | Vism XVIII, `e0102n.mul.xml:5401–5413`; `s0102t.tik.xml:5155` |
| no free fork | AN 5.129, `s0403m1.mul.xml:3536` |
| no standing from holding a clone: only the formal acts confer it | the Vinaya |
| a repo can be cloned; **a stream cannot**: one owner and heir per history | MN 135, `s0203m.mul.xml:4073` |
| **no purpose in the machine** | below |

**Every real program is for something. This machine is for nothing.** It has no author and no first commit, so no
requirements document and no roadmap; it runs only because its conditions keep running. **Purpose lives only in the
commits:** every intention aims somewhere, one repo at a time. The Dhamma names one purpose worth committing to, the
path's and never the machine's: *"this unshakable liberation of mind is its purpose, its heartwood, its end"* (MN 29,
`s0201m.mul.xml:4296`, Piṭaka). Not a better build: the build stopping because nothing feeds it. ⛔ Never *deleting
the repo*: nothing is destroyed, and no one is annihilated.

**And the simile itself has a purpose: it is a raft.** *"I will teach you the Dhamma as like a raft, for crossing
over, not for grasping"* (MN 22, `s0201m.mul.xml:3052`, Piṭaka).
